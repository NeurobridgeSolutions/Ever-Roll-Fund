Wheelchair and Equipment Grants for Spinal Cord Injury

Last reviewed: November 25, 2026

Insurance often stops short of the wheelchair, cushion, standing frame, or home modification a person with a spinal cord injury needs. This guide explains the usual order of steps and lists national nonprofit programs that give equipment grants to individuals. It is published by Ever Roll Fund, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that makes equipment grants for people with spinal cord injuries.

Before you apply anywhere: get a written quote for a specific item from your supplier or ATP, and get your insurer's decision in writing. Almost every equipment grant asks for both.

Step 1: Go through insurance first

Most equipment grants fund the gap insurance leaves, so they ask for a denial letter or explanation of benefits. File the claim even if you expect a denial.

If Original Medicare denies the item, you can appeal. Original Medicare has five levels of appeal, and the first step, a redetermination, generally must be requested within 120 days of receiving your Medicare Summary Notice (Medicare.gov). Private plans and Medicaid have their own appeal rules, so check your plan documents or ask your supplier for help.

Step 2: Gather what grant programs ask for

  • A written quote or invoice for a named item, with make and model
  • Your insurance decision (denial letter or explanation of benefits)
  • Your injury date and level, and a letter from your physician or therapist if the program requires one
  • Your supplier's contact information - most programs pay the supplier directly, not you

Step 3: Apply to the programs that fit

Some programs allow you to combine their grant with other funding, so check each program's rules. Program details below were checked against each organization's own website in November 2026. Programs change, so confirm current rules before applying.

Frequently asked questions

Is there free money for a wheelchair if you have a spinal cord injury?

Yes. Several nonprofits give equipment grants to people with spinal cord injuries, including Ever Roll Fund, Triumph Foundation, the Bryon Riesch Paralysis Foundation, and the Sterling Thomas Push Push Pray Foundation. Most require that you go through insurance first and have a written quote for a specific item.

Do I need an insurance denial before applying for an equipment grant?

Usually yes. Most equipment grant programs, including Ever Roll Fund, fund the gap insurance leaves, so they ask for your insurer's denial letter or explanation of benefits. If you have no insurance, say so on the application.

Who gets paid when an equipment grant is approved?

Most programs pay the equipment manufacturer, DME supplier, or vendor directly, not the individual. Ever Roll Fund pays the equipment manufacturer or DME supplier directly.

Does the Reeve Foundation give grants to individuals?

No. The Christopher and Dana Reeve Foundation states that it is unable to give financial support to individuals. It funds nonprofits through its Quality of Life grants, and its information specialists can help you find other resources.

How do I appeal a Medicare denial for a wheelchair?

Original Medicare has five levels of appeal. The first step is a redetermination request, which you generally must file within 120 days of receiving the Medicare Summary Notice. See Medicare.gov for the forms and steps.
